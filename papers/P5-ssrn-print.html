The Governance Impossibility Map: Five Impossibility Domains in AI Governance and Where the Possibility Domain Begins

SynomosAI Governance Line · Working paper v0.3 (EN) · 2026-09-08
Abstract. Between 2025 and 2026, AI governance witnessed a dense cluster of impossibility results: the information-theoretic limits of explainability (Rao 2025), the computational limits of behavioural governance (McCann 2026), the axiomatic limits of collective accountability (Tibebu & Shemtaga 2026), the structural impossibility of observation-based ex-ante authorization (Meyman 2026; Fernandez 2026; Zhu & Leonard 2026), and the earlier philosophical critique of algorithmic clarity (2019–2025). These results are scattered across five mutually non-citing literature clusters — Tibebu & Shemtaga self-describe as "the first impossibility result in AI governance" although Rao's regulatory impossibility theorem predates theirs by nearly a year, with no citation in either direction. Governors face not a scarcity of theorems but an absence of coordinates. This paper proves no new theorem. It (1) states each of five impossibility domains precisely; (2) offers a nesting-and-overlap integration map, flagged as the authors' synthesis; and (3) locates existing governance assets inside the possibility domain, converting impossibility literature from a knell into a design constraint. The map is the first integrated chart and the first survey-level treatment of this literature.
Keywords: AI governance; impossibility theorem; explainability; undecidability; accountability; ex-ante authorization; determination debt

§1 Introduction

Jobin, Ienca and Vayena (2019) charted the convergence of AI-ethics principles across two hundred guidelines and became the coordinate system for the field — the proof of value for the mapping move: a map adds no new knowledge, but turns existing knowledge into something navigable. Between 2025 and 2026 a symmetric literature appeared: work proving that certain governance goals cannot be jointly achieved. Rao (2025) proves a regulatory impossibility theorem from algorithmic information theory. McCann (2026) establishes the two boundaries of behavioural governance via Rice's theorem and a Coq-mechanized development. Tibebu and Shemtaga (2026) prove an accountability-incompleteness theorem by axiomatic means. Meyman (2026), Fernandez (2026) and Zhu & Leonard (2026) independently establish that observation cannot constitute ex-ante authorization.

The shared predicament is the absence of coordinates: different disciplines, different vocabularies, zero cross-citation. A checkable detail: Tibebu and Shemtaga (2026) call their result "the first impossibility result in AI governance," while Rao (April 2025) had already proved a regulatory impossibility theorem nearly a year earlier; neither cites the other. This is not criticism — it is proof that the map between these results is missing. This paper supplies it.

Naming and demarcation. We call this chart the Governance Impossibility Map. Demarcations: (i) unrelated to the design-theory notion of "impossible maps"; (ii) "regulatory impossibility results" is Rao's coinage, used only to refer to his theorem. No abbreviation is used for the map anywhere in this paper.

§2 Five Impossibility Domains

2.1 Domain ① — Philosophical layer: the algorithmic-clarity critique (critique-grade)

A structural tension exists between the closedness of algorithmic systems and law's demand for determinacy; demanding full legal restatement of algorithmic decisions may be a category error (Kalulé 2019; Springer 2019; IJSL 2025, "Against Algorithmic Clarity"). Boundary: erupts when governance requires complete restatement for legal procedure. Outermost ring — an impossibility at the level of language and concepts.

2.2 Domain ② — Explainability layer: the regulatory trilemma (information-theoretic grade)

Rao (2025, arXiv:2504.20676) formalizes explanation as a simpler approximation of a model, measured by Kolmogorov complexity. A complexity-gap theorem: any explanation significantly simpler than the model must differ from it on some inputs. A regulatory impossibility theorem: no governance framework can simultaneously pursue unrestricted AI capabilities, human-interpretable explanations, and negligible error. Boundary: infeasible only when all three goals are pushed to their limits. Middle ring — an impossibility at the level of information: explanation, as lossy compression, has a mathematical floor.

2.3 Domain ③ — Determination layer: the two boundaries of behavioural governance (theorem-grade)

McCann (2026, arXiv:2604.27292): every system has an expressiveness boundary and a governance boundary; independently defined they yield three regions — governed capabilities, ungoverned capabilities (risk), and governance addressing non-existent capabilities (theatre). By Rice's theorem, for any Turing-complete architecture "this program's effects comply with the governance policy" is undecidable — behavioural governance fails structurally in the general case. The exit is coterminous governance: an architectural decision separating computation from effect, making governance checks part of the execution pipeline (Coq-mechanized: 454 theorems, 36 modules, 0 admitted). Inner ring — semantic-level compliance determination is undecidable unless the architecture changes. Nearest-neighbour demarcation (must-cite): Chalupa's Decision Demonstrability framework and Decision Closure Framework (2026, Zenodo) work the decision-execution axis — when a decision may be executed and who holds authority when legitimacy fails; Domain ③ works the computability axis; Domain ④ the social-choice axis. Chalupa offers no determination debt, no formalizability spectrum, no jurisdictional mapping.

2.4 Domain ④ — Accountability layer: the accountability horizon (theorem-grade)

Tibebu & Shemtaga (2026, arXiv:2604.07778) formalize human–agent collectives within a structural causal model and axiomatize accountability by four minimal properties: Attributability, Foreseeability Bound, Non-Vacuity, Completeness. The accountability-incompleteness theorem: when compound autonomy exceeds the Accountability Horizon and the interaction graph contains a human–AI feedback cycle, no framework can satisfy all four axioms; the impossibility is structural — transparency, audits, and oversight cannot resolve it without reducing autonomy. Below the threshold legitimate frameworks exist: a sharp phase transition (3,000 synthetic collectives, zero violations). Inner ring, responsibility side.

2.5 Domain ⑤ — Authorization–observation layer: observability is not ex-ante authorization (theorem-grade)

Three independent discoveries within one half-year, mutually non-citing — the hardest evidence for coordinate absence. Meyman (2026, FERZ, Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.19647542): no observability architecture can produce an artifact satisfying an ex-ante authorization requirement (three lemmas: causal posteriority of observational signals; non-verifiability of observational artifacts; composition-independence; corollaries of latency and intervention independence). Fernandez (2026, Agent Governance Series): attestation is necessary but never sufficient — integrity and coverage must be separated. Zhu & Leonard (2026, Applied Sciences 16(10):4917): under a fixed information structure, perfect post-deployment monitoring cannot resolve ambiguity intrinsic to the observation structure. Together with Domain ③ they prohibit the governance illusion of substituting a bolted-on observability stack for an explicit authorization/determination architecture.

Output-state nearest neighbour (must-cite): the Learning-to-Defer lineage (Chow 1957 → Madras et al. 2018 → Mozannar & Sontag 2020 → Oxford survey 2025) studies handing over to a human when the AI is uncertain — superficially isomorphic to suspension. Demarcation: L2D is a per-prediction routing action (a statistical learning objective with no institutional semantics); institutional suspension is an institutional state carrying ledger fields, audit chains, debt aging and lifecycle gates. In one sentence: L2D answers "route to whom"; institutional suspension answers "who is accountable during the suspension, how much debt accrues, and when reconsideration triggers." The phrase institutional suspension is unoccupied at phrase level.

Institutional lineage anchor (asset): advance rulings (WTO TFA; CBP binding rulings), declaratory judgment (28 U.S.C. §2201; Abbott Labs v. Gardner 1967), FDA Pre-Sub/Q-Submission; the MDUFA dual-clock stop-clock-on-deficiency mechanism is the official precedent for suspension accounting (roughly 70% of first-round 510(k)s receive deficiency letters — suspension is the norm); MDUFA VI's draft "Not Grantable" letter is a terminal output-state precedent. BPMN escalation events show suspension as a mature engineering mechanism — without debt semantics or sector metrics.

2.6 Periphery (not mapped)

Palm (2026, LinkedIn) — the regulatory incompleteness thesis: no theorem-grade proof; kept as a peripheral note signalling that impossibility narratives are spreading through industry. On Controllability of AI (arXiv:2008.04071, 2020) — a cross-domain compilation of control-theoretic classics; demarcated as an intellectual precursor of the observational dimension, predating the 2025–2026 burst and not covering the map's increment.

§3 Nesting and Overlap (Author Synthesis)

Three integrative claims: (1) Convergent direction, independent routes — five routes (conceptual critique, information theory, computability theory, axiomatic social choice, authorization architecture) approach the same conclusion: governance effectiveness has a structural boundary; the convergence of independent routes is itself evidence. (2) Nesting is synthesis, not consensus — the philosophical layer is outermost; explainability and determination sit inside side by side; accountability overlaps determination under high autonomy plus feedback cycle; authorization–observation sits beside determination. (3) Two empirical symptoms of non-citation — Rao/Tibebu on priority; Meyman/Fernandez/Zhu & Leonard on isomorphic independent discovery. Across this literature even "who was first" and "how many domains exist" lack coordinates; the necessity of a public coordinate system is proven by the literature's own behaviour. §4 The Possibility Domain

Assets are located by a four-question test: Does your governance action rely on semantic-level determination (③)? Does your accountability promise presuppose Completeness (④)? Does your transparency promise ignore the information floor of explanation (②)? Is your observability being sold as authorization capability (⑤)? Assets surviving all four remain valid after the impossibility literature. Worked instance (authors' portfolio): the doctrine of governance determinability (L0–L4 spectrum; determines declared attributes, not program semantics — avoids ③); LGD-I registered identity anchoring (secures Attributability alone — the only anchor region inside Domain ④, since Tibebu concedes legitimate frameworks exist below the horizon); the ACD family determinators (discrete declared attributes in, four-state verdicts with explicit suspension out); XCGS evidence chain (records ≠ verdicts — on the lawful side of Domain ⑤'s demarcation); LGD-III gated (stands on the same side as Domain ⑤'s theorem); A³/PCC progenitor constraint (source-side feedback before the threshold); coexistence doctrine (error shared institutionally, responding to Domain ②). The method generalizes to any regime.

§5 From "Perfect Compliance" to "Debt Management"

A paradigm whose default goal is perfect compliance pours resources into impossibility domains. The turn: the effective output of governance is determination and debt management — (1) explicit determination points on discrete decidable attributes (the exit of Domain ③; the Rules-as-Code movement — Catala, L4, defeasible reasoning, Hildebrandt's critique — supplies the system and its limits); (2) explicit suspension with ledger entries — the dominant failure mode is the silent accretion of determination debt, the stock of declared requirements that never acquired determination conditions; (3) explicit boundaries — every governance promise states which impossibility domain it assumes itself outside of. Demarcation (the debt family): technical, verification (CACM), assurance (Frontiers), compliance (vendor/Gartner), regulatory (medtech parlance) — determination debt is the sixth sibling and the only one pointing at the determination itself: their upstream cause. Cite with explicit demarcation.

§6 Limitations and Replies

(1) The nesting is philosophically contestable — accepted, flagged as synthesis; the map's value is the coordinate function. (2) Boundary conditions may be relaxed by future work — each domain states its boundary condition; a relaxed theorem shrinks a domain rather than invalidating the map. (3) The folk thesis is unmapped for lack of theorem-grade evidence. (4) This negates nothing: the map's purpose is to withdraw resources from impossibility domains and redeploy them where governance works — impossibility domains are design constraints, not a terminus, still less a reason for inaction. (5) Self-positioning is not circular: the map stands independently; §4 is one application instance and the method generalizes; the doctrine is cited as an unpublished working paper.
