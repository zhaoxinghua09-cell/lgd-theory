## A. LinkedIn post (long form)

Every self-governing entity — an AI model, an intelligent device, an autonomous agent — should carry three things across its entire life: a **registry**, **evidence**, and **gates**.

That's the core claim of the **Lifecycle Governance Doctrine (LGD)**, which we are publishing today.

**Why another AI-governance framework?**
Because almost all current efforts are single-point: identity work answers "who is it", audit work answers "can you prove it", alignment review answers "who approved the upgrade". Almost nobody chains these into one unbroken thread from birth to retirement.

**Where does the model come from?**
The most mature lifecycle regulation humans have is **medical-device regulation**: classification → design controls → risk management → registration → clinical evidence → quality system → post-market surveillance → retirement. Every step has requirements, evidence obligations, and release gates. LGD generalizes that reference model to every regulated autonomous thing.

**The three laws**
- **LGD-I · Registry** — register at birth; no exemption for "it's just a small model".
- **LGD-II · Evidence** — every key act leaves an artifact; claims carry source and signature.
- **LGD-III · Gates** — every evolution passes a gate; machines request, humans authorize.

**Already instantiated into a domain series** (each paper maps the domain's own real-world regulatory model onto the three laws): financial AI, electronic evidence chains, digital government, on-chain assets, financial-AI risk tiering, autonomous driving, and data-element lifecycle governance.

**Honest boundaries**: LGD is a civil-society framework, not legal or regulatory advice. Where a lifecycle link has no tooling yet, we say so — we never fake completeness.
